# Research note — Measuring whether a course builds on what it taught (Aug-2026)

> **Frozen snapshot.** Dated field runs (18–19 Aug 2026) and the external inputs that framed
> them. It is the evidence behind ADR-0047 and is not maintained: the vocabulary it reports
> moved twice while the work was in progress, and the counts below belong to the vocabulary
> each section names. Companion: `qa-evals-landscape.md` (Jul–Aug 2026).

The question that opened it: would a knowledge graph, generated by an LLM, give us a
representation of the lessons that makes the remaining criteria of `course-drafts-qa`
executable — above all the ones about conceptual continuity? Efficiency of time and cost was
the axis the question was asked on.

**Short answer: yes to the intermediate representation, no to the knowledge graph.** What the
criteria need is a structured record read by several of them, extracted in one pass — the
extract-then-decide pattern `drafts-hybrid` already uses, raised one grain. A graph proper
(edges, entity canonicalization, communities, a store) costs 5–7× that, and none of the
criteria walks an edge.

---

## 1. What was missing, and what shape it had

The nine criteria the assessor abstained from, with the datum each one actually needs:

| Criterion | Real grain | Datum |
|---|---|---|
| `cr-concepts-introduced-before-use` | course | per lesson: concepts used as already known |
| `cr-later-lessons-apply-earlier-concepts` | course | per lesson: earlier concepts applied |
| `cr-every-objective-is-developed-somewhere` | course | per lesson: objectives developed |
| `cr-every-substantive-lesson-serves-an-objective` | course | the same |
| `cr-lesson-works-at-the-level-its-objective-demands` | lesson | per lesson: cognitive level of the work |
| `cr-new-instruction-is-preceded-by-a-problem` | lesson | per lesson: is there a problem before the material? |
| `cr-skill-lessons-carry-a-worked-example` | lesson | per lesson: skill introduced? shown done? |
| `cr-adjacent-pair-has-a-bridge` | pair | per lesson: does the close announce? does the open assume? |
| `cr-module-single-topic` | module | per lesson: topic |

All nine are served by one per-lesson record of about eight fields. None asks for multi-hop
walks, transitive closures or neighbourhoods: they ask for order comparisons over an index of
lessons, coverage counts over bipartite relations (lesson × concept, lesson × objective) and
local predicates. That is a **table**, not a graph.

## 2. The corpus decides most of the analysis

Measured over the packages on hand: 20 English lessons per course, ~6,700 words, which is
**~9k tokens of lesson bodies for a whole course** — the entire course fits about ten times
into a modern context window.

The motivation of GraphRAG and its family is that the corpus does *not* fit and has to be
indexed to be retrieved. There is no retrieval problem here. The only problem is structure a
verdict can be computed from, which is a different thing.

## 3. External inputs

- **The cost of an LLM-built knowledge graph is dominated by calls per chunk.** GraphRAG makes
  4–6 calls per chunk; indexing one dataset cost US$33,000 in early 2024. The whole later
  evolution of that line consists of *removing* calls — LightRAG does for ~US$0.15 what costs
  GraphRAG US$4–7, and LazyGraphRAG defers the LLM work to query time and reports 0.1% of full
  GraphRAG's indexing cost at equal quality on global questions. The transferable lesson is not
  "use LazyGraphRAG": it is that **calls per unit of text is the cost variable and everything
  else is noise**.
- **Entity canonicalization is the documented failure mode.** EDC (EMNLP 2024) exists because
  open extraction produces semantically redundant nodes — "Nolan" vs "Christopher Nolan". Here
  it would be "selectors" vs "CSS selectors", and because the criterion compares *order of
  first appearance*, an unmerged variant silently invents a violation. Text2KGBench and
  Wikontic report the same.
- **Schema-guided extraction beats open extraction.** SPIRES/OntoGPT (Bioinformatics 2024) is
  the pattern: the model fills a declared shape and every instance is grounded against a
  vocabulary, rather than "extract triples and we will see".
- **In education, the state of the art does not automate the prerequisite graph.** ACE (JEDM)
  ranks concept pairs by semantic similarity and **sends the best ones to a human expert**; its
  contribution is cutting expert effort by 50–97%, not removing it. Automatic methods report
  F1 66.5 (CSR), 59.7 (PREREQ), 51.0 (MOOC-RF); LLM with RAG reaches ~71–72 with lower recall.
  An F1 of ~70 on the prerequisite relation means roughly 3 edges in 10 are wrong, and a
  criterion built on that measures the extractor rather than the generator.
- **On decomposing a judgment.** The most recent controlled study points the other way from the
  worry on record: atomic decomposition **helps on completeness/coverage tasks** and **hurts on
  quality tasks**. It is done over QA with a reference rather than over pedagogical documents,
  so it moves the prior without closing the question.

Sources: LazyGraphRAG (Microsoft Research) · The GraphRAG Cost Cliff · LightRAG · Reduce
GraphRAG Indexing Costs (FalkorDB) · Extract, Define, Canonicalize (EMNLP 2024) ·

## 4. Three options, costed

Basis: 20 lessons per course, ~450 tokens per body, an evaluation of ~10 calls today.

- **A — fan-out per criterion.** Each lesson-grain criterion opening its own call per lesson:
  6 × 20 = 120 calls, plus 19 pairs, plus 8 modules, plus the matrix. Each body is re-sent once
  per criterion: ~150k input tokens per course, nearly all redundant.
- **B — one record per lesson, shared (recommended).** One schema-guided call per lesson
  (~1.5k in, ~500 out) plus 2–3 holistic calls for what genuinely needs the whole course:
  **~23 calls, ~35k input, ~12k output.** Against A: 2–5× fewer calls and 4× less input.
- **C — a full GraphRAG-style knowledge graph.** 4–6 calls × ~25 chunks = 100–150 calls to
  index, plus canonicalization, plus community summaries, plus queries: **5–7× option B**, to
  build a structure no criterion walks, carrying the canonicalization failure mode that breaks
  precisely the criterion that motivated the idea. Architecturally it is also a new port and a
  new adapter for a graph store.

**Cheap non-LLM extractors were set aside**, not rejected on quality: the corpus is small
enough that the absolute saving is cents, serving a local model adds a runtime dependency and a
new axis of assessor identity, and what is being extracted is pedagogy ("is this a worked
example?") rather than entities and relations.

## 5. The turn that made the concept criterion cheap

`course-drafts-qa` v8 mints `lesson_concept_coverage`: the concepts each lesson *declares* it
covers, read off the generator's own plan. With it, the question splits into two halves of very
different cost — where a concept is introduced, and where it is used as known — and the second
becomes a **closed-set** question: of these N declared concepts, which does this body treat as
already understood?

That removes canonicalization entirely: the generator fixes the node vocabulary, the model
never invents a node, it only marks which of a list appear. It is the same move v12 made for
`assessment_verification_match` (cite tasks by number instead of copying text), for the same
reason — take away from the model the work it gets wrong.

The limit is real and belongs in the pass condition rather than in the assessor: only
violations among concepts the generator declared are visible. A concept used and never declared
is invisible.

## 6. Field run 1 — the lesson-major dump (18 Aug 2026)

Three drafts (SOLID 12 lessons / 22 concepts · prompt-engineering 16/27 · REST APIs 12/32).
**The machinery worked; the extraction did not yet yield usable data** — 3 touches, 1 touch,
one dead run, and after a contract fix: dead, 1, 0. The traces named three distinct causes.

1. **The quote came last and the model stopped emitting it.** Three consecutive attempts
   returned `conceptIndex`, `mode`, `newness` and no `quote`. Fixed by putting the quote first
   and stating that a touch that cannot be quoted is not reported.
2. **Schema drift.** The model dropped `lessonId` and answered positionally, or renamed `mode`
   to `type`. With 12–16 lessons nested in one answer, 8–10 lessons ended with no entry.
3. **Quotes spliced with an ellipsis**, discarded silently and in quantity.

Provider note: 10 of 12 draws went to one upstream where the schema drift concentrated — but
that upstream also produced well-formed answers, so the correlation existed and was badly
identified. The denied-provider pool lives in the assessor's identity, so a calibration dump
cannot quietly run a different one, which is the right constraint.

## 7. Field run 2 — two independent readers against the model (19 Aug 2026), lesson-major

Same frozen prompt, SOLID draft, two agent readers working blind — seeing neither the model's
answers nor each other's — and their JSON put through the same validator, grounding and
arithmetic as the model's.

| | touches | earlier/later | uses / re_exp / points | new / same / n_a |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| model high | 40 | 21/19 | 13 / 12 / 15 | 17 / 8 / 15 |
| model high #2 | 29 | 18/11 | 17 / 7 / 5 | 13 / 11 / 5 |
| model medium | 26 | 15/11 | 1 / 22 / 3 | 23 / 0 / 3 |
| reader A | 30 | 18/12 | 19 / 2 / 9 | 13 / 8 / 9 |
| reader B | 34 | 20/14 | 23 / 2 / 9 | 16 / 9 / 9 |

Agreement, measured only where both cite the same passage: **A vs B = 96% on `mode`, 96% on
`newness`**; the model against its own repetition = **55% on `mode`**; medium against the
readers = 23%.

**The task is answerable; the model is the weak link.** Over the 26 touches all three report
and where A and B agree, the model at high effort gets 14. The error is **unidirectional**: 11
cases where the readers say `uses_as_known` and the model says `re_explains`.

Two findings about the **generator**, which both readers reached separately:

1. **Lesson `X.0` already performs the refactor `X.1` supposedly teaches** (modules 02, 04,
   05). That explains the touches that run forward: they are not premature use, the plan's
   concept-to-lesson assignment is finer than the prose.
2. Three concepts are named "Sketching a … Before-and-After Refactor" with no discriminator, so
   attributing a generic mention to one of the three is arbitrary. The closed set has
   indistinguishable members.

## 8. Field run 3 — prompt-eng, two readers and a second model (19 Aug 2026), lesson-major

Eight answers to one prompt over one draft (16 lessons, 27 concepts). `B1` is the bar that
requires a use plus novelty; `B2` is the bar that drops the novelty axis.

| | touches | earlier/later | uses / re_exp / points | B1 | B2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| reader C | 90 | 76/14 | 51 / 10 / 29 | **11/11 PASS** | **11/11 PASS** |
| reader D | 78 | 67/11 | 50 / 10 / 18 | **11/11 PASS** | **11/11 PASS** |
| luna high | 97 | 86/11 | 49 / 44 / 4 | 8/11 PASS | 9/11 PASS |
| luna medium | 80 | 75/5 | 40 / 37 / 3 | 9/11 PASS | 9/11 PASS |
| gemini high #1 | 48 | 47/1 | 30 / 8 / 10 | **5/11 fail** | 9/11 PASS |
| gemini high #2 | 54 | 53/1 | 36 / 8 / 10 | 10/11 PASS | 10/11 PASS |
| gemini med #1 | 34 | 33/1 | 21 / 2 / 11 | 7/11 fail | 7/11 fail |
| gemini med #2 | 26 | 26/0 | 10 / 5 / 11 | 5/11 fail | 5/11 fail |

1. **The PASS is correct.** C and D both say in prose, without seeing each other, that the
   course builds on itself "clearly and deliberately", and both mark 11 of 11 lessons.
2. **The bar must not use the novelty axis.** Two runs of one model at fixed settings give
   **5/11 fail and 10/11 PASS** under `B1` — opposite verdicts — because the novelty axis
   inverts wholesale between runs. Under `B2` every high-effort configuration agrees with the
   readers.
3. **Precision and recall split between the models.** Against the readers on `mode`, gemini
   scores 88–97% and luna 52–60% (luna over-uses `re_explains`), while luna has the better
   recall — 97 touches against 48–54, with the readers at 78–90.
4. **The models are more conservative than the readers at lesson grain** (9–10 of 11 against
   11 of 11), which on a borderline draft could produce a false fail.
5. **Duplicate concept names, confirmed.** Five pairs share a name across lessons; 21 of luna's
   97 touches land on one of those names and in 6 the twin index would give the opposite
   direction. Direction is deterministic given the index, but the index is not determined.

## 9. Field run 4 — the concept axis (19 Aug 2026)

The same corpus asked the other way round: per concept, in which lessons is it defined,
explained, used, assessed or mentioned — **without telling the model which lesson owns which
concept**, joining the plan back on afterwards. Duplicate names merge before asking (27
declared concepts collapse to 22 distinct). prompt-eng draft:

| | appearances | def | expl | used | assess | ment |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| reader E | 111 | 20 | 17 | 54 | 12 | 8 |
| reader F | 99 | 22 | 17 | 40 | 11 | 9 |
| luna high ×2 | 68 / 59 | 20 / 21 | 17 / 9 | 15 / 16 | 14 / 13 | 2 / 0 |
| gemini high ×2 | 53 / 66 | 22 / 22 | 10 / 4 | 12 / 31 | 8 / 8 | 1 / 1 |
| gemini med ×2 | 55 / 80 | 22 / 22 | 6 / 21 | 12 / 20 | 15 / 17 | 0 / 0 |

1. **Where a concept is defined is the most reliable measurement in the whole investigation.**
   E vs F: 20 of 20. Each model against each reader: 19–22 of 19–22 (95–100%). And it comes out
   of the text without looking at the plan — which is exactly the anchor the earlier approach
   took from the plan and which the `X.0`/`X.1` finding shows to be wrong.
2. **Coverage is stable:** all six runs place all 22 concepts, where the lesson-major shape
   varied from 26 to 97 touches with lessons missing entries.
3. **The strict rule does not work; the union does.** With the use bucket alone the verdict
   ranges 3/11 to 11/11 and 2 of 6 runs pass, against readers at 10/11 and 11/11. Taking the
   union of used, explained and assessed: range 7–11, **5 of 6 pass**.
4. **The residue, stated plainly:** the models under-report the use bucket 2–4× against the
   readers (12–31 against 40–54). The union works because it compensates for that missing
   recall — a patch that works, not a solution.

## 10. Field run 5 — SOLID on the concept axis: the criterion discriminates (19 Aug 2026)

Eight model runs (two models × two effort levels × two repetitions) plus readers G and H.

**The criterion separates the two drafts.** Under the union rule:

| | readers | model runs |
|---|---|---|
| prompt-eng (11 lessons in scope) | 11/11 and 10/11 **PASS** | 9, 7, 11, 11, 10, 9 → 5 of 6 pass |
| SOLID (8 lessons in scope) | 5/8 **fail** and 6/8 PASS | 4, 5, 4, 4, 5, 0, 5 → **7 of 7 fail** |

The per-lesson pattern nearly coincides between readers and models: the failures are 02.0, 03.0
and 05.0 — three of the four lessons that open a module.

1. **SOLID is genuinely borderline, and the readers show it.** G and H agree on the reading and
   fall on either side of the bar (5/8 and 6/8, where 6 is needed). In prose both say
   *"partially"*: cumulative inside each module, rhetorical between them. The disagreement is
   about the threshold, not about the facts.
2. **The definition anchor is confirmed on a second draft.** G vs H: **18 of 18**. luna high
   against the readers: 17/17 and 16/16. gemini high against G: 17/17. **luna at medium effort
   drifts to 14–15 of 18** — this axis needs high effort.
3. **The plan-versus-prose signal works.** Definitions placed outside where the plan declares
   them:

   | | reader 1 | reader 2 | models |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | prompt-eng | **0** of 20 | **0** of 22 | 0–1 |
   | SOLID | **5** of 18 | **5** of 21 | 1–6 |

   Two independent readers who never see the plan place zero definitions out of position in the
   draft that reads as cumulative and five in the one that does not. A deterministic, free
   detector of a real defect that no current criterion sees.
4. **gemini high does not survive SOLID.** One run died exhausting a 32k output budget and
   another needed a clean retry (57k tokens for one answer) — more output over a *smaller*
   corpus, which smells of degeneration rather than effort.
5. **The residue is worse here.** The use bucket in SOLID: the models report 0, 0, 1, 9, 0, 0,
   11 against the readers' 14 and 16. The union rule lands correctly because the assessed and
   explained buckets carry the signal when the use bucket collapses. It works, for the wrong
   reason.

## 11. What came out of it

**A model is reliable about coarse, locatable facts and unreliable about which fine category a
fact falls into.** Where a concept is stated, whether a lesson deals with it at all: dependable
enough to build on. Whether the dealing counts as developing it or as using it: not. Build a
criterion on the coarse fact plus a deterministic anchor, never on a fine semantic label.

The corollary, for a dump that disagrees with itself between runs: if the runs report the same
places and label them differently, widen what the criterion counts until the label stops
deciding the verdict; if they report different places, that is recall, and no rewording fixes
it.

Two consequences of method are worth keeping. Reader sets do not survive a change of
vocabulary — every table above belongs to the vocabulary its section names, and the five-group
vocabulary the runbook now describes invalidated the earlier ones. And the readers are agents,
not humans: their convergence says the question is answerable, not that their answer is the
truth.
